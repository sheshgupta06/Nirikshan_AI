1. PROJECT OVERVIEW
1.1 What is Nirikshan AI?
Nirikshan AI is an AI-powered application that analyses photographs of packaged-food labels and provides two separate types of analysis:
Legal Metrology Compliance Module
Food Ingredient Safety Information Module
The application will extract text from product labels, identify relevant declarations and ingredients, compare the extracted information against verified databases and rules, and present understandable findings with supporting evidence.
The system will help users identify possible labelling issues and understand ingredient-related information.
Nirikshan AI is a screening and information-support system. It is not a replacement for laboratory testing, an official government inspection, or a legal certification.
1.2 Problem Statement
Packaged-food labels contain important information, including:
Manufacturer, packer or importer details
Net quantity
Maximum Retail Price (MRP)
Relevant date declarations
Consumer-care information
Ingredient lists
Food additives
Allergen declarations
Other product-related information
Checking this information manually can be time-consuming.
Furthermore, simply extracting text from a label does not establish whether the information is legally compliant or whether an ingredient is permitted under a particular regulation.
Nirikshan AI aims to automate the initial reading and screening process while maintaining evidence traceability and clearly communicating uncertainty.
2. PRODUCT OBJECTIVES
The system must:
Allow users to capture a product-label photograph or upload an existing image.
Extract readable text from the image using OCR.
Improve image quality where appropriate.
Automatically attempt deblurring with Restormer when the configured quality checks indicate that restoration may help.
Analyse mandatory declarations using a verified, database-driven Legal Metrology rule engine.
Identify ingredients using a curated ingredient database.
Present ingredient information and jurisdiction-specific regulatory findings with supporting sources.
Flag uncertain OCR results and ambiguous ingredient matches.
Avoid unsupported claims about food safety or legal compliance.
Provide a responsive, user-friendly interface.
Support deployment as a web application.
Provide a foundation for future research and accuracy evaluation.
3. TARGET USERS
3.1 Consumers
Consumers can use the application to understand packaged-food labels, identify declared ingredients and allergens, and view available regulatory information.
3.2 Inspectors and Reviewers
Potential users include people who need assistance identifying possible labelling issues.
The application must not claim to replace an inspector's professional judgement.
3.3 Data Reviewers
Authorised project administrators or reviewers will maintain the ingredient database, regulatory evidence, and Legal Metrology rule records.
Regulatory records must be reviewed before being treated as verified.

5. USER WORKFLOW
The expected workflow is:
User opens Nirikshan AI.
User chooses Legal Metrology, Ingredient Safety, or both.
User captures a product-label photograph or selects an image from the device.
The application displays an image preview.
The backend validates the image.
The system checks for possible blur, poor lighting, low contrast and other image-quality problems.
OpenCV preprocessing is applied where appropriate.
If Restormer is enabled and the quality policy indicates that deblurring may help, the system attempts restoration automatically.
PaddleOCR extracts text from the original and relevant processed image candidates.
The system compares the OCR outputs and flags important disagreements.
The user is asked to retake the photograph or confirm uncertain text when necessary.
The selected analysis modules process the extracted information.
The application displays findings, uncertainty, and supporting evidence.
The user can review the results and understand their limitations.
The application must preserve the original image during processing.
If restoration fails, the application should fall back to the original image where possible instead of crashing the complete analysis.
6. MODULE A — LEGAL METROLOGY COMPLIANCE
6.1 Purpose
This module will screen packaged-food labels for selected declarations relevant to the Legal Metrology Act, 2009, and the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011, as applicable to the product and packaging.
The precise legal requirements must be verified against the applicable current rules and amendments before implementation.
6.2 Information to Detect
The system should attempt to identify applicable declarations such as:
Manufacturer details
Packer details
Importer details, where applicable
Net quantity
Maximum Retail Price (MRP)
Relevant manufacturing, packing or import date declarations
Consumer-care details
Other applicable mandatory declarations
Not every declaration necessarily applies to every product. The engine must account for the applicable product and packaging scope.
6.3 Processing Requirements
The module must:
Extract label text through OCR.
Identify candidate declarations.
Normalize extracted text for matching.
Retrieve applicable, verified rules from the database.
Evaluate whether the relevant declaration appears to be present.
Flag possible issues and uncertain cases.
Return an explanation and reference to the rule used.
6.4 Finding Categories
The module should distinguish between:
Detected: Relevant text was identified.
Not detected: The system could not find the expected declaration.
Potential issue: Available evidence indicates a possible discrepancy.
Needs manual verification: OCR, applicability or rule interpretation is uncertain.
Not assessed: No applicable verified rule is available.
A declaration not detected by OCR must not automatically be treated as legally absent. It may have been cropped, obscured or incorrectly recognized.
6.5 Font-Size and Text-Height Measurement
Font-height measurement may be implemented as an advanced feature.
However, pixel height cannot reliably be converted into physical millimetres without a validated calibration method and suitable image-capture conditions.
Therefore:
Do not invent physical measurements.
Do not claim legally accurate millimetre measurements from uncalibrated photographs.
Return measurement as unavailable when calibration requirements are not satisfied.
Validate any implemented measurement method against known physical measurements before using it for compliance findings.
6.6 Rule Database Requirements
Every rule record should contain:
Rule identifier
Rule title
Jurisdiction
Product and packaging scope
Rule summary
Source reference
Effective date, where available
Verification status
Last verification date
Review notes
Regulatory thresholds must not be hardcoded into frontend components.
The system must use verified database records and clearly distinguish a potential issue from a definitive legal determination.
7. MODULE B — FOOD INGREDIENT SAFETY INFORMATION
7.1 Purpose
This module will read the ingredient declaration from a packaged-food label and present information about identified ingredients, declared allergens, and relevant regulatory or scientific evidence.
It must not assume that every additive is harmful or that every ingredient absent from a warning database is safe.
7.2 Ingredient Analysis Workflow
The workflow should be:
Extract ingredient text using PaddleOCR.
Identify the ingredient-list section where possible.
Separate candidate ingredient names.
Normalize spelling and formatting.
Match each candidate against canonical ingredient records and verified synonyms.
Retrieve available evidence and applicable regulatory records.
Evaluate whether the match and evidence are sufficiently reliable.
Display the ingredient identity, available information, regulatory context and sources.
Mark ambiguous or unidentified ingredients for confirmation.
7.3 Ingredient Identification
The first version should use:
Exact-name matching
Verified synonym matching
Text normalization
Conservative fuzzy matching
Explicit ambiguous and unknown outcomes
A large language model may help explain retrieved information in simple language, but it must not independently decide whether an ingredient is legally permitted or prohibited.
The system must not automatically accept a fuzzy match as a confirmed ingredient identity.

7.4 Ingredient Information
Where verified information is available, show:

Ingredient type or function

Relevant allergen information

Applicable food category and conditions of use

Only show information supported by the available evidence.
7.5 Regulatory Status
Regulatory status must be evaluated separately for each jurisdiction and applicable use.
The application must distinguish between:
Permitted under specified conditions
Restricted
Prohibited or not authorised under the applicable rule
Unknown
Not assessed
These labels must reflect the wording and meaning of the underlying verified source.
A restriction in another country must not automatically be treated as an Indian prohibition.
Likewise, absence from the database must not be interpreted as proof of permission or prohibition.
7.6 Health-Related Information
The module may explain evidence-backed ingredient information in understandable language.
However:
An ingredient must not be labelled harmful merely because it is an additive.
Scientific findings must be presented with relevant context and limitations.
A general hazard finding must not be presented as proof that the quantity in a specific product is dangerous.
The application must not infer the actual concentration of an ingredient from its name alone.
It must not claim that the product passed or failed laboratory testing.
It must not label an entire product completely safe or unsafe based only on its label photograph.
7.7 Allergen Detection
The module should identify declared allergens when the OCR text and verified ingredient mappings support the finding.
It must distinguish between:
An allergen-related term detected in the label text.
A possible allergen match that requires confirmation.
No allergen identified in the readable text.
Failure to detect an allergen does not prove that the product is allergen-free.
The interface should advise users with serious allergies to verify the physical packaging and relevant manufacturer information.
8. IMAGE PROCESSING AND AI PIPELINE
8.1 PaddleOCR
PaddleOCR will be the primary text-extraction component.
It should extract text and bounding boxes where supported by the selected implementation.
Confidence values must be used only if the selected OCR interface actually provides them.
OCR confidence is not a guarantee of correctness.
8.2 OpenCV
OpenCV will support:
Image decoding and validation
Image orientation handling where possible
Blur estimation
Brightness and contrast assessment
Image resizing
Preprocessing
Suitable thresholding or contrast enhancement
Preparation of image candidates for OCR
Image-quality thresholds must be calibrated using the project's own test images.
8.3 Restormer
Restormer is the optional image-restoration component intended to improve certain blurred images before OCR.
Required behaviour
Assess image quality.
Determine whether restoration should be attempted.
Run the actual Restormer model if enabled and available.
Preserve the original image.
Run OCR on the original image.
Run OCR on the restored image when restoration succeeds.
Compare the outputs.
Use the validated selection policy to choose a result or flag uncertainty.
Request user confirmation when important text differs.
Fall back safely if the model fails.
Restrictions
Do not claim that every blurred image can be repaired.
Do not assume restored text is more accurate.
Do not silently replace the original image.
Do not implement a sharpening filter and describe it as Restormer.
Do not activate the feature in production until the real model is tested in the target environment.
The model implementation, licence, weights, dependencies and hardware requirements must be verified before integration.
8.4 Custom ML Model
A separately trained custom ML model is not mandatory for the MVP.
The initial system can use existing OCR and restoration models, a database-backed matching process, and deterministic rule engines.
Custom training should be considered only after testing identifies a specific weakness that cannot be adequately addressed by the existing components.

9.2 Data Quality Policy
The system must:
Record where every important regulatory claim came from.
Keep unreviewed records separate from verified records.
Track jurisdiction and effective dates.
Avoid duplicate ingredient identities.
Flag outdated sources.
Avoid inventing legal limits or regulatory status.
Support a documented review process.
Return unknown when sufficient verified evidence is unavailable.
A database record should not be treated as verified merely because it was imported successfully.
10. DATA COLLECTION AND ACCURACY VALIDATION
10.1 Ingredient Database Development
The database should be developed through a controlled process:
Collect representative packaged-food label samples.
Record the ingredients actually printed on those labels.
Create candidate canonical ingredient records.
Identify synonyms using credible sources.
Collect relevant official regulatory records and scientific references.
Record jurisdiction, category, conditions and effective dates.
Have the records reviewed before marking them verified.
Test matching against both known and previously unseen ingredient text.
Update the database when reliable new information becomes available.
Do not generate arbitrary ingredient statuses to make the application appear complete.
10.2 OCR Test Dataset
Create a manually verified dataset containing images with:
Clear labels
Blurry labels
Low-light images
Glare and reflections
Curved packaging
Small text
Partially cropped ingredient lists
OCR-sensitive spelling variants
Each test image should have a human-verified transcription for the relevant text.
10.3 Accuracy Metrics
Evaluate the following:
Character Error Rate (CER)
Word Error Rate (WER), where appropriate
Exact-match rate for important declarations
Ingredient matching precision
Ingredient matching recall
Ambiguous/unknown case handling
Legal rule decision accuracy on reviewed examples
False-positive findings
False-negative findings
OCR performance before and after Restormer
Processing time and resource usage
Do not publish accuracy percentages until the corresponding experiments have actually been completed.

17. OUT OF SCOPE
The following are not part of the first MVP:
Continuous live-camera scanning.
Laboratory test simulation or replacement.
Prediction of actual chemical concentration without appropriate measurements.
Guaranteed detection of all adulteration or tampering.
Official government certification.
Definitive declaration that a food product is completely safe or unsafe.
Unverified automatic scraping of legal databases.
Automatic approval of newly imported regulatory records.
Custom model training without a demonstrated need.
Guaranteed recovery of unreadable text by image restoration.
These features must not be advertised as implemented unless separately developed, tested and approved.

The MVP will be considered ready for demonstration when:
A user can capture or upload a packaged-food label photograph.
The application validates the image and provides useful quality guidance.
OpenCV preprocessing operates as intended.
PaddleOCR extracts text from supported images.
Restormer is either genuinely integrated and tested or clearly disabled; it is never simulated.
Original and restored OCR outputs can be compared when restoration is enabled.
Uncertain text is not silently treated as correct.
Legal Metrology findings reference verified applicable rules.
Ingredient matches use canonical records and reviewed synonyms.

Frontend, backend and database integration tests pass.
The application can be deployed and accessed through its configured hosting environment.

20. FINAL PRODUCT PRINCIPLES
All development decisions must follow these principles:
Evidence before conclusions: Important regulatory and ingredient claims must be traceable to credible sources.
Unknown is better than a false answer: Uncertainty must be visible.
Separate OCR from interpretation: Reading text does not establish compliance or safety.
Separate regulatory status from health evidence: These are different questions.
No fake AI: Features must use the actual stated models or be labelled unavailable.
No unsupported accuracy claims: Performance must be measured on reviewed test data.
Preserve original evidence: Image restoration must not silently replace the original.
Human review where necessary: High-impact ambiguous results must be flagged.
Versioned regulatory data: Rules and sources must be reviewed and maintained.
Incremental development: Build and test one module or phase at a time.
Final Definition
Nirikshan AI is a photo-based, AI-assisted packaged-food label screening platform that combines a database-driven Legal Metrology module with an evidence-backed Food Ingredient Safety Information module.
Its purpose is to make label information easier to analyse, identify potential issues, and provide transparent explanations—not to replace laboratory testing, official inspection, or professional regulatory judgement.
